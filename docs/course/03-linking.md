# Linking your thoughts

*Module 1: The basics · about 15 minutes*

Folders put a note in one place. Links put it in every place it belongs. Linking is what turns a
pile of notes into a web of ideas you can wander through.

## Learn

- **Link to a page:** type `[[` and a few letters of the title, then pick it. If the page doesn't
  exist yet, you can create it from the same list.
- **Link to a block:** type `((` and a few words of a paragraph or heading anywhere in your
  workspace. The link points at that one block.
- **Backlinks:** the right panel lists every page that links to the one you're on, with the
  sentence the link is in.
- **Unlinked mentions:** pages that mention this page's title without linking it. One click finds
  connections you didn't make yet.
- **Tags:** write `#idea` or `#book` anywhere in your text. The **Tags** view in the rail lists
  every tag and the pages that use it. Pages can also get tags as a property (lesson 6).
- **The graph:** the right panel draws this page and the pages around it (**Local**), or your
  whole workspace (**All**). Click a dot to open that page.
- **Sub-pages as blocks:** `/page` makes a new sub-page and puts a link to it in your text.

## Try it

- [ ] On your first page, type `[[` and link to "Projects".
- [ ] Open "Projects" and find the link in **Backlinks** on the right.
- [ ] Type `[[A page that doesn't exist yet]]` and create it from the list.
- [ ] Link to a single paragraph with `((`.
- [ ] Add `#learning` to two pages, then open **Tags** in the rail and click it.
- [ ] Switch the graph between **Local** and **All**, and open a page from it.

> [!TIP]
> Don't worry about where a note belongs. Write it down, link it to what it's about, and let
> backlinks and the graph find it for you later.
